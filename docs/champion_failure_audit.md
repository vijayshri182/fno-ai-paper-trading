## 2. Why MA(5,21) lost 143% — the decomposition

### 2.1 Gross edge does not exist even before friction

| Layer | Per trade | t-stat | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross P&L at bar-close fills (no costs) | **+1.12** | **+0.66** | Not distinguishable from zero |
| Realized P&L at slippage-adjusted fills | **-42.09** | **-24.60** | ≈25σ below zero |
| Per-trade friction | **-56.18** (slippage -43.22 + commission -12.96) | — | 50× the gross edge |

`edge / cost = 0.020`. Even a *zero-cost* scenario returns only +2.92% (all friction-free
scenarios are reviewed in the model-performance report's cost-sensitivity table). The
signal has **no measurable gross timing edge** (t=+0.66 on 2,601 independent crossovers),
and slippage alone (₹43.22/trade) is 38× that edge.

### 2.2 The dominant structural finding: a churn problem, not a direction problem

The champion's realized trades this window were **0 long entries / 2,601 short entries**
(verified at trade level and via `pair_round_trips`; the alternating crossover stream began
with a SELL while the engine only ever entered when flat, so every BUY was a close). It
short-sold every dip and covered every bounce into an index that rose +34% — the
classic **"sell the dip, buy the rip"** churn against an upward drift.

Danger is concentrated in **holding duration**, all of it verified at trade level:

downside trend-following ability; whipsaw churn drowns it.

- The 2% stop-loss fired **2 times in 2,601 trades** — crossovers close positions long

## 3. Root-cause summary

1. **No gross edge**: crossover timing at bar-close fills is +₹1.12/trade (t=+0.66).
2. **Churn**: 2,601 trades / 1,128 days (2.3/day); median hold ~1 hour; 47% of signals
   flip within 50 minutes.
3. **Counter-trend shorts into drift-up**: all entries were shorts; the small winners
   (97 holds) could not offset 1,840 sub-20-bar losses.
4. **Friction = 50× edge**: slippage ₹43.22 + commission ₹12.96 per trade.
5. **Stop/regime tools do not rescue it**: stop fired twice; every regime bucket negative.
